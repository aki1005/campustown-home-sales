Data & Methods

Price rules

Analysis medians and the dashboard summary use sale prices of at least $10,000 and no more than $5,000,000, inclusive. This excludes implausibly low and high values documented in the data description. Dashboard filter prices use the same bounds unless a user sets different controls.

Days on market

Days on market is contract_date - list_date, calculated only when both dates are available and parseable. sale_date - list_date is closing time, not days on market, and missing dates are excluded rather than treated as zero.

Missing data

Unknown property-feature values are kept as unknown. They are not converted to No. Living areas equal to 0 or blank are excluded from the living-area range. The 127 zero living-area values and other source blanks are documented in the data description. Repeated addresses and parcel-number cases remain distinct rows identified by listing_id.

Dataset citation

College-town home sales dataset, version 1.0 (2026). University of Illinois-Deloitte Foundation Center for Business Emerging Technologies. Data collected March 2026. Source values were not independently verified against county records. Read the data description sheet.

P1. Data Quality

Issue: Missing values in list_date or contract_date must not be interpreted as zero days. A blank date means the required date is unavailable.

Across the full dataset, 7,400 of 35,442 records (20.9%) lack at least one date needed to calculate days on market. Treating each blank as zero produced a mean of 26.86 days and a median of 6.0 days, while incorrectly assigning zero to 7,784 records. After excluding incomplete records and calculating contract_date − list_date only where both dates exist, 28,042 records (79.1%) remain. The cleaned mean is 33.95 days and the median is 11.0 days; only 384 listed and contracted same-day sales remain at zero. Thus, improper zero substitution understates the median by 5.0 days and makes the distribution appear much faster than it is.

The missing dates are not distributed randomly. Pittsboro has the highest missing-date rate in the table at 38.0%, followed by Iowa City at 34.6%, State College at 32.8%, Carrboro at 28.4%, and Charlottesville at 25.9%. This missingness matters because a city-level days-on-market statistic describes only sales with observable timing. Excluding incomplete records is therefore transparent and necessary, although it may omit homes sold off-market or through new-construction transactions. A conclusion should state this eligible-record limitation rather than treating unavailable dates as instant sales.

P2. Working with a Coding Agent

Building the Q1–Q5 College-Town Home Sales website with GitHub Copilot made the development process more efficient than starting every component from scratch. Copilot helped produce the HTML structure, JavaScript calculations, SVG charts, tables, and explanatory text. It also helped turn the assignment questions into independent visual analyses, including the Q1 city comparison, Q2 mean–median gap, Q3 rank comparison, and the campus-area charts in Q4–Q5. I did not perform a separate pandas or SQL analysis for this project, so I cannot claim a measured time saving, but generating and revising these components in one website was more convenient than manually writing each part.

The results still required careful verification. For example, an early CSV parser used a simple comma split; I noticed that quoted fields containing commas could break it and changed the parser. I also checked the specified price ranges, date requirements, listing_id rule, and the instruction not to deduplicate street addresses. I independently recalculated results, checked the browser output, and reviewed GitHub Actions when deployment problems appeared. Not every agent task was smooth: some tasks stayed loading or returned errors, so progress could not be assumed from the agent’s status alone.

Compared with pandas or SQL, Copilot is stronger for connecting analysis to a readable interface and visual explanation, while traditional tools offer more direct, reproducible data checking. Copilot can also misunderstand requirements or introduce subtle calculation errors. I prefer Copilot for this project because it combines coding and presentation, but human judgment remains essential: I must inspect the data, verify calculations, and confirm that the final site answers the assignment rather than merely looking convincing.
